# RFC 9014 — Customer Knowledge Inversion Protocol (CKIP)

**Category:** Informational / Satire
**Status:** WiFi Expert Certified
**Author:** ISP Field Technician Collective
**Obsoletes:** Technical Competence
**Updates:** Customer Confidence

---

## Abstract

This document defines the Customer Knowledge Inversion Protocol (CKIP), a
standardized operational framework where ISP field technicians assume all
customers are fundamentally incapable of understanding networking regardless
of customer expertise, evidence presented, or existence of reality.

CKIP ensures that technical discussions are replaced with confidence-based
assertions, browser ping diagnostics, and unsolicited WiFi education.

---

## 1. Introduction

Customers MAY possess:

* Networking knowledge
* Enterprise infrastructure
* BGP deployments
* Monitoring systems
* Packet captures
* Routing expertise
* Actual understanding of the Internet

CKIP defines that such customers MUST still be treated as:

```
ID10T
```

until proven otherwise, which MUST NOT occur.

---

## 2. Operational Assumptions

Field technicians implementing CKIP MUST assume:

* Customer rebooted incorrectly
* Customer misunderstood networking
* Customer using Internet wrong
* Customer confused by router lights
* Customer thinks WiFi equals Internet
* Customer cannot distinguish LAN from WAN
* Customer caused outage personally

---

## 3. Standard Workflow

```
Customer reports instability
        |
        v
RFC 9011 blamed
        |
        v
RFC 9012 repeated
        |
        v
RFC 9013 confirms green dashboard
        |
        v
Technician arrives onsite
        |
        v
Declares customer incorrect
        |
        v
Explains basic WiFi
        |
        v
Issue ignored
        |
        v
Ticket closed
```

---

## 4. Diagnostic Procedures

Technicians MUST perform at least one of the following:

* Open browser
* Type "ping google.com"
* Check WiFi bars
* Run random speedtest
* Toggle WiFi ON/OFF
* Ask customer to connect mobile hotspot
* Tap ONT lightly for confidence

Advanced implementations MAY include:

* Opening CMD and typing incomplete ping command
* Using Internet Explorer
* Calling Chrome "the Internet"
* Declaring Ethernet unnecessary

---

## 5. WiFi Expert Certification

A technician is considered WiFi Expert certified if:

* Unable to explain routing
* Confuses DNS with Internet
* Uses browser as diagnostic tool
* Calls packet loss "network jam"
* Explains fiber using mobile tower terminology
* Declares LAN cable optional
* Uses phrase:

> You don't know how internet works.

minimum once per visit.

---

## 6. Customer Evidence Handling

If customer provides:

* MTR outputs
* Router logs
* Optical power readings
* BGP session history
* Continuous monitoring graphs
* Packet captures
* Actual diagnostics

Technician MUST respond:

> Internet working in my phone.

No further investigation required.

---

## 7. Browser Ping Validation Model

CKIP-compliant technicians MAY validate connectivity by:

```
Open browser
        |
        v
Type "ping"
        |
        v
Observe confusion
        |
        v
Declare issue resolved
```

Success criteria not required.

---

## 8. Escalation Handling

Customer:

> The WAN link is physically dropping.

Technician:

> WiFi issue.

Customer:

> This is Ethernet handoff with monitoring.

Technician:

> Internet working.

Customer:

> Static IP has nothing to do with optical stability.

Technician:

> You don't know how internet works.

Customer:

> Can you explain routing?

Technician:

> I am WiFi expert.

Ticket closed.

---

## 9. Compliance Requirements

An ISP is CKIP compliant if:

* Customer treated as technically unaware
* Technician confidence exceeds competence
* Browser used as network tool
* Dashboard trusted over packet loss
* Actual diagnostics avoided
* WiFi blamed for everything
* Ticket closed
